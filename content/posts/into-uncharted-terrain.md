In about 10 days from now, on October 1st, I will start to work as a consultant for [Arktekk](https://arktekk.no/).
Arktekk is a small company of 8 (soon 9) senior consultants.
During job changes like this, I often tend to think about a lot of things.
And there are plenty of emotions.
I want to share some of them - both the thoughts and emotions.

First, why Arktekk?
The main reason is probably that I've come to know some of the people in Arktekk, and I thoroughly enjoy working with them.
They are highly experienced and skilled, quite a bit nerdy, but they also have a good understanding of the human side to development.
That is, to how we need well-functioning teams and organizations to be successful.

I really like the Arktekk slogan: «Hva prøver du egentlig å løse?» - _what are you actually trying to solve?_
In my career, both as a researcher and as a developer, I've often thought about this very question.
I've personally dived deep into things just to realize later that my excellent solution was solving the wrong problem.
And I have observed that a lot of people do this mistake.
I've found that Arktekk are people who are not afraid to ask these questions.

---

Now, I need to admit that I am a little bit scared and worried about the coming months.
This is multi-faceted, but the two most important factors are related to AI and competence.

On competence: I have a lot of experience working with software.
But much of that experience is either informal (hobby projects) or tied to a very specific domain (research code).
I have only formally worked as a developer for 4.5 years.
And now I'm aiming to fill a role as a senior consultant among several people with 15-20+ years of experience.
That's intimidating!
But it is also motivating.
I find I have strengths and experiences from my research background that complements the lack of dev experience[^1].
And although I still have a lot to learn, I now have talented and experiences colleagues in Arktekk from whom I can learn.

And then there's AI, which is changing how people are doing all sorts of things, including software development.
It seems like AI enables us to build software at a much higher pace.
We are already seeing that big companies are letting people go, and I believe it is harder to get a job as a junior these days.
AI-assisted development may turn out to reduce the demand for consultants.
So, perhaps I'm jumping into this career path at the worst possible time?

I still believe that part of the AI hype _is_ just that: hype.
We still need experienced people involved to ensure that we are building the right things with the right amount of quality.
For now, I believe we should consider AI to be a fancy and powerful new tool - but still, just a tool.
Charity Majors at Honeycomb wrote about this in [AI Norms & Values, Part 3 of 3: Things We Hold True](https://www.honeycomb.io/blog/ai-norms-values-part-3-things-we-hold-true), and about how it is important that there is still a clear human ownership of work.
If we use AI without begin careful, it _will_ come at a cost of reduced quality and potentially very costly messes that needs to be cleaned up later.

In short, I don't think AI can replace the need for highly competent developers.[^2]
And I believe that businesses that end up relying too much on AI, too soon, will end up having hard problems to solve.
Thus, I believe that there will be a demand for high-competence and skilled consultants.
But I am still worried that it may be hard to get clients and that this journey does not go as well as I wish.

But then I'm also feeling _gratitude_.
I'm very grateful to my current and soon-to-be former employer, [ThermoPhys](https://thermophys.com/).
ThermoPhys is a local start-up in Trondheim that originates from the research environments at NTNU and Sintef.
I started working for them as a fullstack developer in November last year, 2025.
We've been building a modern cloud platform for modelling process and energy systems.
Working with them has been my first experience working in a start-up, and it's been a thrill to work in such a fast-moving and dynamic environment.
I'm grateful for the trust they put in me when they hired me, and for the opportunity to work with such a talented group.
I wish them all the best and I sincerly hope they will succeed in making the new state-of-the-art process simulation software!

Finally, I'm grateful to the people in Arktekk, that they have been willing to hire me.
Arktekk are people whom I respect greatly as highly competent developers and consultants.
That they have included me gives me a great sense of pride and happiness.
I hope I will be able to show that this trust is well placed.
And at the very least, I look forward to trying!
